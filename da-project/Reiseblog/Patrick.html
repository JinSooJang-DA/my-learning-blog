I’m Ethan, a curious traveler from Germany who likes discovering the tales hidden behind familiar places. I love historic streets, local details and spontaneous detours. For me, each journey is a chance to slow down, learn something new and share it with others.

Founded by the Romans more than two thousand years ago, Augsburg is among Germany’s oldest cities. Its historic centre brings together Renaissance facades, quiet courtyards and lively squares. At the heart of it all stands the grand Town Hall, designed by Elias Holl, beside the medieval Perlach Tower.
A walk along Maximilianstrasse reveals monumental fountains, merchant houses and reminders of the wealth once created here by the Fugger and Welser trading families of Europe.

Rain levels in Augsburg

Augsburg has a temperate climate, with rain possible throughout the year. Summer usually brings the warmest days as well as short, heavier showers, while spring and autumn are often mild and changeable. Winter is cold, with occasional snow adding a special atmosphere to the old town. Because the weather can shift quickly, a compact umbrella and an extra layer are useful in each season. Even on rainy days, museums, churches and historic interiors offer plenty to explore too.

Popular water activities

Augsburg has no tropical beach, but its water shapes many notable activities. Follow the Lech canals through the old town, visit the monumental fountains or cycle beside the rivers. In summer, locals can also relax at nearby lakes and outdoor pools.

Accommodation expenses

Augsburg offers central hotels, cozy guesthouses and modern apartments. Rooms near the old town cost more, while outer districts provide quieter and cheaper stays.

Augsburg’s water management system became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019. Its network includes canals, water towers, fountains, waterworks and hydropower plants. The city separated drinking water from process water as early as 1545, centuries before modern hygiene research explained why this mattered. Another highlight is the Fuggerei, founded by Jakob Fugger in 1521 and known as the world’s oldest social housing complex. Residents still pay a symbolic annual rent and agree to pray for the founder’s family. Fine-art lovers can explore the Schaezlerpalais, while theatre fans can trace playwright Bertolt Brecht, born here in 1898. With Roman roots, Renaissance splendour and lush river landscapes, Augsburg rewards guests who explore slowly.
